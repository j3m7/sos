NAME
    grep - only the lines of a command's output that hold a text

SYNOPSIS
    <command> | grep [-v] [-i] [-n] <text>

DESCRIPTION
    Passes on the lines of the command's output that hold the text, and drops
    the others ('dmesg | grep wifi', 'cat /log/sos.log | grep error | more').
    The text is a fixed string, not a pattern: no regular expressions and no
    wildcards. It is everything after the options, spaces inside it kept
    ('grep no route' looks for "no route"), at most 64 bytes.

    -v    the lines without the text instead
    -i    upper and lower case alike (ASCII letters only)
    -n    each line starts with its number in the output and a ':'

    Options can be joined ('-vn'). A word starting with '-' that isn't made of
    these letters begins the text ('grep -x' looks for "-x").

    Lines are passed on whole, as soon as each one ends; a last line without
    a newline gets one. A line longer than 512 bytes is matched in pieces of
    512 bytes, each one on its own (and shown as a line of its own); with -n
    the pieces share the line's number.

NOTES
    grep works only after a '|': typed on its own it says so. A '|' starts a
    stage only when a stage name follows it (grep, head, tail, wc, more), so
    the text may hold a '|' that doesn't ('grep a|b'). Error messages of the
    command go through grep like its other output. More about pipes in
    'man more'.
