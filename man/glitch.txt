NAME
    glitch - the RP2350 glitch detector: setting, state and resets

SYNOPSIS
    glitch
    glitch on <0-3>        (console only)
    glitch watch <0-3>     (console only)
    glitch off             (console only)
    glitch default         (console only)
    glitch trig            (console only)

DESCRIPTION
    The chip's glitch detector resets it when its clock or core supply is
    disturbed, which is what a fault-injection attack does. 'glitch' shows its
    setting, whether it is armed now, how many times it has reset the board
    since power-on, and for the last such reset which detectors fired, how
    long the board had been up and the chip's temperature then (the last
    reading before the reset, taken every 10 s).

    'glitch on <0-3>' arms it by software, now and at every boot, at that
    sensitivity (0 lowest, 3 highest). 'on 3' is the default: a board is armed
    at the highest sensitivity unless set otherwise. This is not protection:
    it is armed a second or two into boot and software can disarm it; its
    purpose is to see whether the detector resets a healthy board before it is
    enabled for good in OTP. If it resets the board three times in a row
    within three minutes of starting, the next boot leaves it unarmed and says
    so. A detector reset loses the fault record.

    'glitch watch <0-3>' runs the circuits at that sensitivity without arming
    them: a trigger is logged with the chip's temperature and shown by
    'glitch' ("armed, it would have reset the board"), nothing is reset. A
    detector that has triggered may stay triggered until the next reset, so
    each of the four is reported once per boot.

    'glitch off' disarms it, now and at boot: the way out if it resets a
    healthy board. 'glitch default' goes back to armed at 3. 'glitch trig'
    makes one detector fire, to check that a detector reset is seen and
    reported; it resets the board if the detector is armed.
