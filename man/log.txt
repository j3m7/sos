NAME
    log - the log levels, the log files on the card and their rotation

SYNOPSIS
    log
    log console <level>           (console only)
    log history <level>           (console only)
    log file <level>              (console only)
    log rotate                    (console only)
    log rotate every <N>d         (console only)
    log rotate at <HH:MM>         (console only)

DESCRIPTION
    Three levels decide what the log does with a message: 'console' is what
    the serial console prints as it happens (default notice), 'history' what
    is kept in memory for 'dmesg' (default info), 'file' what is written to
    the log files on the card (default info). Levels, from most to least
    important: err, warning, notice, info, debug. A level change lasts until
    reboot and is itself logged (facility auth) before it applies.

    'log' shows the levels and the log files' state: writing, no card mounted
    (messages wait in the history), or stopped after a failed write (with the
    file and the error); lines and bytes written this boot, bytes waiting to
    be written, messages dropped from the history before they could be
    written; and the rotation: how often and when, the last and the next.

    Every message the history keeps is also written to the card, one file per
    facility: /var/log/kern.log, auth.log, daemon.log, user.log, and syslog
    for messages without a facility. Lines read

        2026-11-26T14:03:12.345Z notice console: logged in

    (UTC; '+12.345', seconds since boot, while the clock isn't set), and each
    boot starts with a '--- boot: SOS <commit>, last reset: <why> ---' line.
    Warnings and errors are written at once, the rest within 5 s. Without a
    card, messages wait in the 4 KB history and a mount is tried every minute
    (not after 'sd unmount'); once a card is in they are written, with a line
    saying how many were dropped meanwhile. A failed write stops the files
    until the card is mounted again.

    Rotation: every 7 days at 00:00 UTC by default, each file becomes
    <name>.1 (replacing the previous one) and a new one starts, so the card
    holds the current period and the one before. 'log rotate' rotates now;
    'log rotate every <N>d' (1 to 365) and 'log rotate at <HH:MM>' change
    when, saved in the configuration. The time of the last rotation is kept
    on the card (/var/log/.rotated), so a reboot keeps the count and a
    rotation missed while the board was off happens at the first chance;
    without the clock set nothing rotates.

    Like /certs, /var/log can be read from anywhere (SSH, SFTP, Lua) but
    changed only on the console, so a session that gets in can't erase its
    traces; /var itself is protected too.
