NAME
    time - the clock, the RTC and time sync over NTS

SYNOPSIS
    time
    time sync
    time sync sntp                      (console only)
    time auto [<hours> | off]           (change: console only)
    time cal [<ppm> | off]              (change: console only)
    time server [<name> | default]      (change: console only)

DESCRIPTION
    All times are UTC. The RTC (a PCF8523 on the Adalogger, with its coin
    cell) is the source of truth: at boot the clock is set from it. Network
    time comes from NTS (Network Time Security, authenticated NTP): a TLS key
    exchange with the NTS server on port 4460, then NTP queries that only
    accept replies that check out under the server's key. There is no
    fallback to plain NTP: without NTS and without an RTC time, the clock
    stays unset.

    'time' shows the date and time and where it came from (RTC, NTS or SNTP),
    the RTC's reading and backup-battery state, the calibration, the drift
    the last sync measured, the last sync with its correction and round trip,
    a failed attempt and when it is retried, the server and the periodic
    setting.

    'time sync' syncs the clock and the RTC from NTS now. The board also syncs
    by itself when the RTC has no valid time (retrying with doubling delays),
    and periodically if 'time auto <hours>' (1 to 720; default off) is set:
    one interval after the last successful sync, or after boot if there has
    been none. A correction of more than 5 minutes is logged as a warning.
    'time sync sntp' does one plain, unauthenticated SNTP sync on request.

    'time server <name>' sets the NTS server (host name or IPv4 address);
    'default' goes back to the build-time one (time.cloudflare.com). Its
    certificate must chain to a trusted root ('tls roots').

    The 'drift:' line: every sync reads the RTC just before setting it, and
    compares it with the time it was set to at the sync before (kept in flash
    across restarts and power cuts). So after two syncs it shows the RTC's
    rate, e.g. 'rtc +92.0+-13 ppm in 22 h (no cal)': positive means fast, and
    the figure after +- is how far off the measurement may be (the RTC counts
    whole seconds, so the longer between the syncs, the better: about 15
    hours for 20 ppm). When the two syncs fell in one uptime it also shows the
    board's own crystal ('xosc'). The 'rtc-drift' health check judges both.

    'time cal <ppm>' corrects the RTC's rate: 'time sync', a day or more,
    'time sync' again, then give the rate 'drift:' shows, e.g. 'time cal 92'
    (the 'rtc-drift' check's warning suggests the value). The RTC is slowed
    or sped up by the nearest step of 4.34 ppm and 'time' shows the rate
    applied ('cal: slowed by 91.1 ppm'). The value replaces the previous one:
    with a calibration set, give the rate shown on the 'cal:' line plus the
    new drift. The next sync after a change starts a new measurement. 'time
    cal off' removes it. Saved, and written into the RTC at boot if the chip
    doesn't have it.
